01 / Why IRD exists

The space between
the idea and the document.

People have a technical problem or a product idea, but no document that organizes the problem, compares alternatives and makes a decision possible. IRD exists to fill that space.

It is not a consultancy with a consultancy structure or a construction company. It is a technical documentation studio: I analyze, compare and document. Execution, prototypes and sign-off stay with whoever is responsible for them.

02 / How I work

Four principles.
No shortcuts.

The method does not depend on the size of the project. Depth changes; the way of thinking about the problem does not.

01

The problem before the solution

First we define what is happening, why and what evidence exists. The solution comes later.

02

Alternatives before opinion

At least three paths compared by cost, timeline, risk and standards. The recommendation is explained.

03

Declared assumptions

Every calculation depends on assumptions. They are written down, tested and marked as such.

04

Explicit limits

The document says what it does not include. That protects the client, the signing professional and the implementer.

03 / What I do and don't do

What I deliver
and where scope ends.

Clarity about scope is part of the service. If your project needs a signature, a prototype or construction work, I tell you before starting and help place the document in that flow.

I don't sell a signature I don't have or experience I can't prove. I sell the analysis and documentation work that a licensed professional can review and sign, and that your team can use to decide.

Scope: The document delivers analysis, alternatives and a technical viability conclusion; professional validation and sign-off are handled by a licensed professional when appropriate.

What I do

  • Analyze the problem with your data.
  • Compare alternatives using explicit criteria.
  • Document the solution and its limits.
  • Deliver the final document as a PDF.
  • Support validation with your professional.

What I don't do

  • Sign as a licensed engineer.
  • Execute the work or manufacture the equipment.
  • Build physical prototypes for clients.
  • Manage construction or coordinate contractors.
  • Guarantee implementation results.
04 / Professional validation

The signature is not
part of the deliverable.

The document saves the professional from rebuilding the problem from scratch. It arrives with data, alternatives, calculations and limits; their job is to review, adjust and sign when regulations require it.

That flow is not fine print. It is how the document gains technical value and how professional responsibility stays where it belongs.

If you don't have a professional to validate the document, I can help identify the right profile, but I do not replace that review or sign in their place.
