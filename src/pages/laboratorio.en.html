Modular biomass valorization system

Organic waste such as pruning, used oil or shells often ends up in final disposal, with a cost and no energy recovery. The question is whether that waste can become a dense, usable fuel through a simple, measurable process.

It is not a success case. It is an open project with hypotheses, assumptions and public next steps. When something is not solved, it is stated.

01 / The problem

Waste that costs
and energy that is missing.

In many industrial and agricultural processes, residual biomass is treated as a cost: it has to be removed, stored or disposed of. At the same time, the operation buys energy or fuel to dry, heat or move equipment.

The hypothesis is that part of that waste can be densified and thermally valorized in small modules, with biochar as a by-product. It is not a promise of savings: it is a hypothesis to verify with balances and data.

02 / The hypothesis

Collection, densification and valorization modules.

The concept is a modular chain: waste collection, moisture reduction, densification, thermal valorization and by-product management. Small scale makes it possible to test in parts, but it also imposes limits on performance and logistics.

The research does not seek to prove that the system works everywhere. It seeks to identify under what conditions it makes sense and what needs to be measured before investing.

03 / Status

What is solved.
What is missing.

The 45% progress is an estimate of work, not a technical validation. The following is what is not closed yet.

What is solved

  • Problem framing and system boundaries.
  • Densification alternatives compared at a conceptual level.
  • Preliminary regulatory requirements identified.
  • Process and by-product hypotheses defined.
  • Research questions prioritized.

What is missing

  • Technical feasibility of small-scale presses.
  • Mass and energy balance with real data.
  • Emissions and biochar management.
  • Investment and operating costs.
  • Tests with different waste and moisture levels.

The next milestone is the mass and energy balance. It has no public date until there is enough data to set one.
